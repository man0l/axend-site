All of these are cosmetic tools for adults 18 and over, and none can say anything definitive about how attractive you are.

## The short version

- **Highest checklist score: The Face Report (9 of 10).** The formula is shown, the free scan runs in your browser, and pricing is clear. The useful part, what to fix, is behind a $17.49 report.
- **Axend scored 8 of 10, second.** Strong on privacy and method, weaker on cost and on what the free web scan tells you. The full plan needs the iOS app and a subscription.
- **PFPMaker scored 5 of 10 and AttractivenessTest.com 4 of 10.** Both give a flattering number with little explanation.
- **The numbers do not agree.** For one AI-generated man, AttractivenessTest.com said 9.9 out of 10 ("one in a million") and Axend said 7.6.

## How we tested

**Date:** November 10, 2026. **Browser:** a clean desktop Chromium session with no account and no saved cookies, driven by a script so each tool received the same files in the same way.

**Test photos.** Three AI-generated portraits that ship with this site (Photo A, a woman; Photo B, a man; Photo C, a second woman). They are synthetic images of people who do not exist, so no real person's face was uploaded to any third party. All three are front-facing, evenly lit and neutral.

**What we recorded for every tool:** the URL, the price, where the photo is processed, what the result shows, a summary of the privacy policy, and the score for each of the three photos. To check where the photo goes, we logged the network requests the page made while the scan ran, and looked for an upload of the image itself.

**How we scored the checklist.** Each tool gets 0, 1 or 2 on five criteria, for a maximum of 10:

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Privacy | Photo goes to third-party AI services or is unclear | Uploaded to the tool's own server, or policy is vague | Not uploaded in the free scan, and the policy says so |
| Method transparency | A number with no explanation | Lists what it measures | Shows the formula or weights |
| Honesty about limits | Claims accuracy or reads traits it cannot measure | Limits only in small print | Limits stated next to the result |
| Cost | Hidden or recurring charge | Free preview, but the price is unclear or recurring | Free scan and a clear one-time or free price |
| Actionable advice | Score and a one-liner | Names a weak area, details paid | Specific, changeable steps in the free result |

## The tools, one by one

### 1. The Face Report

![The Face Report result for Photo A: 83 out of 100, with symmetry 94 and the formula shown](/guides-tests/thefacereport.webp)

- **What it shows:** a 0 to 100 score, a percentile against its own scan cohort, and four measured terms (symmetry, facial thirds, facial fifths, length-to-width) with the weights and the arithmetic written out. For Photo A it showed 94 × 0.35 + 70 × 0.25 + 87 × 0.25 + 72 × 0.15 = 83.
- **Photo handling:** landmarks are found in the browser. Our network log showed one small JSON message (about 6 KB) with measurements and no image upload. Its privacy policy says the photo is not uploaded for the free geometric tools, but that other tools (age, skin, color, full report) send photos to Google's Gemini API, and the written report is generated with Anthropic's Claude.
- **Price:** the free scan is free. The full report is $17.49 once, with no subscription and a 7-day refund.
- **Honest about limits:** yes, it labels the number "not a beauty, health, or clinical score" and notes that its percentile compares against its own self-selected scan cohort.
- **Weak spot:** the free result tells you your "highest-leverage area" but locks the detail.

### 2. Axend (ours)

![Axend result for Photo A: baseline 7.8 out of 10, symmetry 9.4, proportions 9.2, jawline 4.4](/guides-tests/axend.webp)

- **What it shows:** one baseline out of 10 and three traits (symmetry, proportions, jawline) in plain bands: Strong, Balanced, Room to improve. The [method page](/how-we-score) publishes the weights: 40% symmetry, 30% proportions, 30% jawline.
- **Photo handling:** on your device. The only request our log saw during the scan was the host's page-analytics beacon. No image left the page.
- **Price:** the web scan is free. The app is $4.99 per week or $49.99 per year.
- **Honest about limits:** yes. Its own method page says the thresholds are heuristic and not calibrated against a large sample, and that one photo is a noisy sample.
- **Weak spots:** this is where we lose points, and the table shows it.
  - **iOS only today.** There is no Android app yet, so the full plan is not available to Android users.
  - **The free scan is thin.** It covers three geometry traits. Skin, hair, posture and smile are in the app, not on the web.
  - **The paid plan is a subscription,** and $4.99 a week is more than the one-time prices of the two report-based tools above.
  - **On-device models are lighter** than server models, and our thresholds are first-pass choices.
  - **All three test photos got a low jawline score** (4.4, 4.4 and 3.1) even though they are studio portraits, which shows how much that one trait depends on lighting and head angle.

### 3. PFPMaker Attractiveness Test

![PFPMaker result for Photo A: 8.2 out of 10, Top 10% worldwide, PSL 5.5, golden ratio 1.56, symmetry 96%](/guides-tests/pfpmaker.webp)

- **What it shows:** a score out of 10, a "Top 10% worldwide" badge, a PSL rating, a golden-ratio figure, a symmetry percentage and a face-shape label, plus a named "biggest drag" (here, cheekbones).
- **Photo handling:** the face model loads and runs in the browser. Our log showed one small (under 1 KB) result message to a PFPMaker subdomain and no image upload. Its privacy policy is a general policy for all its tools: it says some tools process images without storing them and that images processed without an account are generally not retained beyond the session. It does not describe the attractiveness tool specifically.
- **Price:** the free preview is free. "Continue my analysis" moves you to a separate report page; we did not see a price before that step.
- **Honest about limits:** partly. A line under the heading calls it "for entertainment only", but the result uses labels like "Flawless" and "Top 10% worldwide" with no cohort explained.
- **Note:** the three photos got 8.2, 7.9 and 7.9, and gave three different face-shape labels (Round, Square, Heart).

### 4. AttractivenessTest.com

![AttractivenessTest.com result for Photo A: score 8.8, top 13%](/guides-tests/attractivenesstest.webp)

- **What it shows:** a score out of 10, a "Top N%" badge, a detected gender and a one-line verdict such as "You could easily be a model". A "Show detailed breakdown" button is available; the headline gives no explanation of how the number was made.
- **Photo handling:** uploaded. Our log showed the image sent to its server in a form post (about 100 KB). Its policy says photos are held in memory only for processing and are not saved or shared, except where you use Share/Download or buy a Pro report. The page also loads a large amount of ad-tech.
- **Price:** the score is free. The Pro Face Report is US$9.97 once (shown as reduced from US$50).
- **Honest about limits:** weakly. "For entertainment purposes only" is in small print under a result that told us Photo B was "one in a million".
- **Note:** this tool gave the highest numbers by a wide margin: 8.8, 9.9 and 8.2.

## What the results show

- **Different tools, different scales.** For Photo B the scores were 7.6 out of 10, 7.9 out of 10, 84 out of 100 and 9.9 out of 10. The ordering of our three photos also changed from tool to tool.
- **Flattering numbers sell reports.** The two tools with the highest scores and the biggest labels ("Flawless", "one in a million") were also the two with the least explanation. We cannot prove cause, and we note the pattern only.
- **Explained numbers are more useful even when they are lower.** A tool that shows its formula lets you see which input moved the score.

## A checklist before you use any tool

1. Can I find out where my photo goes? If not, skip it.
2. Does it explain what it measures?
3. Does it say what it cannot measure, such as skin, hair, expression and charisma?
4. Is the price clear before I commit, and is it a one-time or recurring charge?
5. Does it suggest realistic steps such as haircut, grooming, posture and sleep, rather than miracle fixes?
6. Does it avoid medical claims and diagnosis?
7. Does it state an adults-only policy?
8. How does the score change if I retake the photo? A good tool gives similar results with the same setup.

If a tool fails several of these, treat its score as entertainment.
