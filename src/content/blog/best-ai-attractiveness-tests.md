Instead of ranking brands, which would go stale quickly and would be hard to do fairly, this guide compares categories of tools and gives you a checklist for judging any of them. All of these are cosmetic tools for adults 18 and over, and none can say anything definitive about how attractive you are.

## Category 1: On-device scanners

These run a face model in your browser or on your phone. The photo is processed locally and not sent to a server.

**Strengths**
- Strong privacy, since the image stays on your device.
- Usually fast and often free.
- Often show measurements such as symmetry, thirds and jaw angle.

**Limits**
- Depend on device capability, so models are usually lighter.
- Measure geometry only, with the usual limits from angle, lighting and expression.
- Advice may be basic unless paired with an app.

## Category 2: Upload-and-score web tools

You upload a photo, a server processes it, and you get a score.

**Strengths**
- Can use larger models.
- Simple to use, with nothing to install.

**Limits**
- Your photo leaves your device. Check retention, sharing and training policies.
- Methods are often opaque, and scores may be tuned to feel flattering or to push you to pay.
- Many give a single number with no explanation.

## Category 3: Chat-based AI

General-purpose AI assistants that accept images and can comment on your face.

**Strengths**
- Flexible, can discuss haircut, grooming and photo tips in plain language.
- Can answer follow-up questions.

**Limits**
- Not designed to measure proportions, so numbers are unreliable and inconsistent between runs.
- Answers may be overly flattering or generic.
- Photos are uploaded, so check the provider's data policy.
- Tends to produce confident-sounding text about things it cannot actually measure.

## Category 4: App-store apps

Dedicated apps that include scans, routines and trackers.

**Strengths**
- Can combine a baseline with a plan, reminders and progress tracking.
- Good for ongoing habits.

**Limits**
- Subscriptions and paywalls are common, so read pricing carefully.
- Quality varies widely, and some lean on hype or insecurity to convert users.
- Check permissions and privacy labels before installing.

## Criteria that matter

| Criterion | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Privacy | Clear statement on whether photos are uploaded, stored, or used for training |
| Transparency | Explains the method and shows sub-measurements |
| Accuracy limits | States plainly that scores are approximate and photo-dependent |
| Cost | Clear pricing, no surprise paywall after the scan |
| Actionable advice | Points to things you can change, with realistic expectations |
| Tone | Honest, not flattering or shaming, no medical claims |

## A checklist before you use any tool

1. Can I find out where my photo goes? If not, skip it.
2. Does it explain what it measures?
3. Does it say what it cannot measure, such as skin, hair, expression and charisma?
4. Is the price clear before I commit?
5. Does it suggest realistic steps such as haircut, grooming, posture and sleep, rather than miracle fixes?
6. Does it avoid medical claims and diagnosis?
7. Does it state an adults-only policy?
8. How does the score change if I retake the photo? A good tool gives similar results with the same setup.

If a tool fails several of these, treat its score as entertainment.

## Final advice

Use the same photo setup every time, and compare changes over weeks, not minutes. Pay more attention to which habits help than to any one number. If a result affects your mood, put it down and talk to someone you trust.
