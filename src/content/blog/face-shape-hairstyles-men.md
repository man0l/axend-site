Haircut advice organised by face shape is popular because it works as a starting framework. The idea is simple: use hair to balance proportions, adding length where a face is wide, adding width where it is narrow, and softening or sharpening edges. Treat it as a guide, not a rulebook.

## How do I find my face shape?

Pull your hair back, stand square to a mirror and take a straight-on photo at eye level. Compare the widths of your forehead, cheekbones and jaw, then compare your length with your width. The widest point and the jaw shape point to oval, round, square, oblong or heart.

1. Pull hair away from your face and stand square to a mirror.
2. Take a straight-on photo at eye level.
3. Compare the **forehead width** at the temples.
4. Compare the **cheekbone width** at the widest part of the face.
5. Compare the **jaw width** at the angle of the jaw.
6. Compare the **length** from hairline to chin with the width.

Then match what you see to the closest of these shapes:

- **Oval:** Longer than wide, with a gently rounded jaw. Balanced proportions.
- **Round:** Width and length close, soft jaw and cheeks.
- **Square:** Strong, angular jaw, forehead and jaw similar in width.
- **Oblong:** Noticeably long face, straight sides.
- **Heart or triangle:** Wider forehead tapering to a narrower chin.

Many faces sit between two categories. That is normal.

## How do haircuts balance face shape?

Haircuts work through four ideas. Height adds length. Sides add or remove width. Texture softens or sharpens edges. A fringe changes the forehead. Use them to even out proportions, not to hide your face. Treat the idea as a guide, not a rulebook.

Haircuts work through a few principles:

1. **Height adds length.** A lifted top makes a round face look longer.
2. **Sides add or remove width.** Tight sides slim a wide face, fuller sides widen a narrow one.
3. **Texture softens or sharpens.** Crisp lines emphasise angles, textured finishes soften them.
4. **Fringe changes the forehead.** A fringe shortens a long face, a swept-back style opens it.

## Which haircut suits each face shape?

Oval faces take most styles. Round faces want height and short sides. Square faces suit short, classic cuts. Oblong faces want less height and some fullness at the sides. Heart faces want a fringe or forward-textured top. The table gives the best cuts and what to avoid for each shape.

| Shape | How to spot it | Best cuts | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oval | Longer than wide, gently rounded jaw, balanced proportions | Most styles: textured quiff, side part, crop, slick back. See [oval face hairstyles](/guides/oval-face-hairstyles-men) | Very tall tops or a heavy low fringe |
| Round | Width and length close, soft jaw and cheeks | Height and shorter sides: textured quiff, high fade with volume, side part. See [haircuts for round faces](/guides/haircuts-for-round-face-men) | Heavy side volume, flat buzz cut |
| Square | Strong angular jaw, forehead and jaw similar in width | Short classic cuts such as a crop or side part. A slightly longer textured top softens angles | Heavy blunt cuts that stack more angles on a strong jaw |
| Oblong | Noticeably long face, straight sides | Medium length with fringe, or sides that keep some fullness | A very high quiff, which exaggerates length |
| Heart | Wider forehead tapering to a narrower chin | A fringe or forward-textured top, slightly more length at the sides, a short beard for jaw weight | Styles that push all the volume to the top of the forehead |

### Oval

Most styles work. You can experiment with length. Our guide to [oval face hairstyles for men](/guides/oval-face-hairstyles-men) goes deeper.

### Round

Aim for height and shorter sides: a textured quiff, a high fade with volume on top, or a side part. Avoid heavy volume at the sides. See [haircuts for round faces](/guides/haircuts-for-round-face-men) for details.

### Square

The strong jaw already gives structure. Short, classic cuts such as a crop or side part suit it. Slightly longer textured tops soften the angles if you prefer.

### Oblong

Avoid adding lots of height. Medium length with fringe, or sides that keep some fullness, reduce the length. A very high quiff exaggerates it.

### Heart

A fringe or forward-textured top balances a wider forehead. Slightly more length at the sides gives the lower face more presence. A short beard can also add jaw weight.

## What matters besides face shape?

Hair texture, density, hairline, lifestyle and facial hair carry equal weight. Choose a style that works with your natural pattern. Thinning hair usually looks best shorter and tidier. A cut you cannot style in two minutes will be abandoned. A beard changes the effective face shape, so plan hair and beard together.

- **Hair texture:** Curly, wavy and straight hair behave differently.
- **Hair density and hairline:** Thinning hair usually looks best shorter and tidier. Fighting a receding hairline with a comb-over rarely works. If hair loss worries you, see a doctor or dermatologist.
- **Lifestyle:** A cut you cannot style in two minutes will be abandoned.
- **Facial hair:** A beard changes the effective face shape, so plan hair and beard together.

## How do I talk to my barber about face shape?

Bring two or three reference photos, ideally of men with similar hair and a similar face. Describe the length on top and sides in practical terms. Ask how long the style takes and what product it needs. If you do not like the result, say so kindly and ask what can be adjusted.

For example, ask for "tight on the sides, enough on top to push back."
