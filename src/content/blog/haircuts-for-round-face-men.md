A round face has width and length that are close to equal, with soft cheeks and a rounded jaw. The goal of a haircut is not to hide this, since there is nothing to hide, but to add vertical length and a bit of structure so the proportions look more balanced. Here is how.

## How do I know if I have a round face?

A round face has a width at the cheekbones that is close to its length from hairline to chin. The cheeks are soft and the jaw curves instead of angling. Pull your hair back and take a straight-on photo to check.

![Chart of five outline face shapes labelled oval, round, square, oblong and heart](/guides-img/faceshape-chart.png "The five face shapes: oval, round, square, oblong and heart")

A defined jaw suggests a square face, and a broader overview is in [face shape and hairstyles for men](/guides/face-shape-hairstyles-men). If your face is clearly longer than it is wide, our [oval face guide](/guides/oval-face-hairstyles-men) fits better.

## What is the goal of a haircut for a round face?

The goal is not to hide roundness but to add vertical length and some structure. Add height on top, keep the sides tight, and use texture and angles. Lifted hair makes the face look longer. Side volume adds width, which is what you want to avoid.

1. **Add height on top.** Lifted hair makes the face look longer.
2. **Keep the sides tight.** Volume at the sides adds width, which is what you are trying to avoid.
3. **Use texture and angles.** Sharp parts, defined lines and a textured finish counter softness.

## What are the best haircuts for a round face?

A textured quiff, a pompadour, a side part with a fade, a faux hawk or a longer top swept back all work. They share height on top and short sides. The table says why each works and what to ask your barber. Details on each follow below.

| Cut | Why it works | Ask your barber for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Textured quiff | Height without stiffness | Short sides, two to three inches of textured length on top, pushed up and slightly back |
| Pompadour | A more dramatic lift | A fade on the sides, and a styling plan you will really follow |
| Side part with a high or mid fade | The part adds an asymmetrical line that breaks up roundness | A clear part and narrow sides |
| Faux hawk or textured spikes | A strong vertical line | Height down the centre, kept understated |
| Longer top, swept back | Adds length if hair is thick enough | A light-hold product so it does not look plastered |

### Textured quiff

Short sides with two to three inches of textured length on top, pushed up and slightly back. It adds height without looking stiff.

![Illustration of a young man with a textured quiff, tousled top swept up and back with short tapered sides](/guides-img/cut-textured-quiff.png "Textured quiff")

### Pompadour or modern pomp

A more dramatic lift. Works well with a fade on the sides. Needs more styling time and product, so be honest about whether you will do it daily.

![Illustration of a young man with a modern pompadour with a voluminous swept-back top and faded sides](/guides-img/cut-pompadour.png "Pompadour")

### Side part with a high or mid fade

The part adds an asymmetrical line, which breaks up the roundness. A fade keeps the sides narrow.

![Illustration of a young man with a side part on top and a mid skin fade on the sides](/guides-img/cut-side-part-fade.png "Side part with a mid fade")

### Faux hawk or textured spikes

Height down the centre and short sides give a strong vertical line. Keep it understated, not extreme.

![Illustration of a young man with a faux hawk, textured top lifted at the centre with faded sides](/guides-img/cut-faux-hawk.png "Faux hawk")

### Longer top, swept back

If your hair is thick enough, brushing it up and back creates length. Use a light-hold product so it does not look plastered.

![Illustration of a young man with a longer top swept back from the forehead with short sides](/guides-img/cut-swept-back.png "Longer top, swept back")

## What fade is best for a round face?

A mid or high fade with a clean taper keeps the width down. Low fades can work if you keep enough height on top. Avoid blunt cuts that leave bulk above the ears. Narrow sides let the top do the work of adding length.

## What haircuts should a man with a round face avoid?

Avoid a flat, even buzz cut, a heavy straight fringe, big side volume and long hair that falls to the cheeks. Each one adds width or removes height. A buzz cut is fine if you like it, but it does not add structure. The table shows what to try instead.

| Avoid | Why | Try instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat, even buzz cut | Shows the whole round shape with no vertical element | Short sides with some height on top |
| Heavy, straight fringe | Cuts the face horizontally and shortens it | A part or a swept-back top |
| Big side volume | Adds width | Tight sides and a clean taper |
| Long hair that falls to the cheeks | Frames the roundest part of the face | Keep length on top, not at the sides |

## Does a beard suit a round face?

Often yes. A short beard that is fuller at the chin and trimmed shorter on the cheeks suggests a longer, more defined lower face. Keep the cheek line neat and avoid beards that widen the sides. If you cannot grow a full beard, clean stubble or a clean shave is fine.

## What else matters besides face shape?

Hair texture and density matter as much as shape. Curly hair adds width, so keep the sides tight and the curls on top. Thinning hair usually looks better shorter, and a doctor or dermatologist can advise on hair loss. Body fat changes how round a face looks, but a haircut does not require weight loss.

## What should I tell my barber?

Bring photos of men with a similar hair type. Ask for short sides, plus length and texture on top. Ask how to style it and what product to use. Book trims every three to four weeks, because fades grow out quickly.

## How do I check whether the cut works?

Photograph the new cut after a few days, in daylight, from the front and the side. Compare it with your old photos.
