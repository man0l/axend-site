Looking better is mostly a stack of small, boring improvements. No single trick transforms a face, but a handful of habits, done consistently, change how you come across in person and in photos. This guide puts them in order of effort versus payoff.

You are an adult working with the face and body you have. The goal is not to become someone else. It is to present your own features cleanly and consistently.

## Start with the cheap, fast wins

### Haircut

A good haircut is the highest-leverage change for most men. It frames the face, balances proportions, and hides or highlights specific areas. Pick a barber you trust, bring a reference photo, and ask what suits your head shape and hair density. Get it refreshed on a regular schedule, usually every three to five weeks for shorter styles.

If you are not sure where to start, [face shape and hairstyles](/guides/face-shape-hairstyles-men) is a good primer.

### Facial hair or a clean shave

Decide deliberately. Patchy, unshaped stubble tends to look unfinished. Either keep a short beard with clean neck and cheek lines, or shave properly. Trim nose and ear hair, and keep eyebrows tidy by removing only strays between the brows.

### Clothes that fit

Fit matters more than price. Shoulders should sit at your shoulder edge, sleeves should end near the wrist, and trousers should not pool at the ankle. A tailor can fix a lot for a small cost.

## Build a basic skin routine

Skin is one of the most visible things on a face. The basics are modest: a gentle cleanser, a moisturiser, and daily sunscreen. Add one active ingredient at a time only if you need it, and give it several weeks before judging. Persistent acne, painful breakouts, or changing moles are medical matters, so see a dermatologist or doctor rather than guessing.

## Work on posture and body composition

Posture changes how tall, confident and open you look. Slumped shoulders and a forward head also hide the jaw and neck line. Regular mobility and back-strengthening work helps, but expect gradual change. See [posture exercises](/guides/posture-exercises) for a simple routine.

Body-fat level affects how defined the face and jaw look. Sustainable eating, regular strength training and walking are the unglamorous route. Sleep matters too, since tired faces look puffy and dull. Do not crash diet or chase extremes.

## Smile and expression

A relaxed, natural smile reads better than a forced one. Keep up with dental hygiene and regular check-ups. If you are unhappy with your teeth, ask a dentist what is appropriate for you. Practising in a mirror helps you learn which expression feels natural rather than staged.

## Think about lighting and photos

Much of what people call looking bad is bad photography. Soft light from a window, a camera at eye level, and a slight chin-forward angle already improve most pictures. This is worth a separate effort, covered in the photo guide.

## Make a simple plan

Try this order:

1. Fix the haircut and facial hair.
2. Get clothes that fit.
3. Start a three-step skin routine.
4. Add ten minutes of posture work most days.
5. Build sleep, activity and food habits you can keep.
6. Retake a photo under the same conditions every month.

Tracking helps, because slow changes are hard to see day to day. Take the same photo in the same light each time and compare after a month or two.

## What is realistic

Some things are fixed: bone structure, height, and the broad proportions of your face. Others are very changeable. Be honest about which category a concern falls into, and spend your effort on the second.

Do not expect a number or a routine to define your worth. Appearance is one part of how people see you, and not the biggest. Energy, humour and how you treat people count for a great deal.
