An oval face is often described as the easiest to style, because its proportions are already balanced. That does not mean any haircut will look good, but you have more freedom than most. This guide explains what an oval shape looks like and how to use that freedom well.

## How do I know if I have an oval face?

An oval face is about one and a half times as long as it is wide. The forehead is slightly wider than the chin, the cheekbones are the widest point and the jaw curves gently instead of forming sharp corners. Many men sit between two shapes, and that is fine.

If your face is closer to equal in width and length, it may be round, and [haircuts for a round face](/guides/haircuts-for-round-face-men) will suit it better. If it has a defined square jaw, it may be square. Many men are somewhere in between, and that is fine. For a wider overview, see [face shape and hairstyles for men](/guides/face-shape-hairstyles-men).

## What should an oval face avoid?

Avoid styles that push the proportions off. Very high volume on top adds length. A heavy, low fringe shortens the face and hides the forehead. Heavy side volume widens it. Where other shapes need correction, an oval face needs maintenance, so within those limits choose by taste.

## Which hairstyles suit an oval face?

Most do. A textured quiff, side part, textured crop, slick back or medium-length cut with movement all work. Keep the top lifted but not towering, and keep the neckline clean. Choose by hair type, lifestyle and how often you can get a trim. The table says why each works.

| Cut | Why it works | Ask your barber for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Textured quiff or pompadour | Moderate height keeps the balance without adding too much length | Tighter sides, textured top lifted but not towering |
| Side part | Shows the forehead and suits business and casual settings | A classic side part, kept neat |
| Textured crop | Short, easy to style and shows a balanced face | A light fringe forward that does not hide the forehead |
| Slick back | Shows the whole face, so it suits balanced proportions | Enough length to sweep back, if your hair is thick enough |
| Medium length with movement | Relaxed and works with wavy or straight hair | A few inches, swept back or to the side, with a clean neckline |

### Textured quiff or pompadour

Moderate height with texture works. Keep the top lifted but not towering. Tighter sides keep it neat.

### Side part

A classic, versatile cut. It shows the forehead and suits both business and casual settings.

### Crop or textured crop

Short, easy to style, and shows off a balanced face. A light fringe forward adds some character without hiding the forehead.

### Slick back

Works well if hair is thick enough. It shows the whole face, so it suits men comfortable with proportions being visible.

### Medium length with movement

A few inches of wavy or straight hair, swept back or to the side, looks relaxed. Keep it clean at the neckline.

## Which fade is best for an oval face?

Almost any fade works. A low or mid fade is the safe choice, and a high fade is more dramatic. Go for the one that matches your style and how often you can get a trim. Tighter sides also keep a lifted top looking neat.

## Which beard suits an oval face?

Most beards suit an oval face. A short, well-groomed beard or stubble works well. A very long beard can extend the face. If you want to add a little jaw definition, keep the cheek line clean and the neckline tidy.

## What matters more than face shape for a haircut?

Hair texture, density, hairline and upkeep often matter more. Fine, straight hair needs different cuts from thick, curly hair. A receding hairline usually looks best with shorter hair. Choose a cut that grows out well. For hair loss concerns, ask a doctor or dermatologist.

- **Hair texture and density:** Work with what you have.
- **Hairline:** Shorter hair usually suits a receding hairline.
- **Upkeep:** A great cut that grows out badly within two weeks is not great. Choose something that ages well.
- **Styling product:** A small amount of matte paste or clay is enough for most styles.

## How do I ask my barber for the right cut?

Bring two or three photos of cuts you like and describe the length on top and sides. Ask for a style that grows out well. Wait a few days before judging, since fresh cuts can look too sharp or stiff. Then photograph the result under normal light.
