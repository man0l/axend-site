An oval face is often described as the easiest to style, because its proportions are already balanced. That does not mean any haircut will look good, but you have more freedom than most. This guide explains what an oval shape looks like and how to use that freedom well.

## What an oval face looks like

Check these points in a straight-on photo with your hair pulled back:

- The face is about one and a half times as long as it is wide.
- The forehead is slightly wider than the chin.
- Cheekbones are the widest point, but only slightly.
- The jaw curves gently rather than forming sharp corners.

If your face is closer to equal in width and length, it may be round. If it has a defined square jaw, it may be square. Many men are somewhere in between, and that is fine. For a wider overview, see [face shape and hairstyles for men](/guides/face-shape-hairstyles-men).

## The goal: preserve balance

Where other shapes need correction, an oval face needs maintenance. Avoid styles that push proportions off:

- Very high volume on top adds length, which can make the face look long.
- A heavy, low fringe shortens it and can hide the forehead that already balances the face.
- Heavy side volume widens it.

Within those limits, choose by taste.

## Styles that work well

### Textured quiff or pompadour

Moderate height with texture works. Keep the top lifted but not towering. Tighter sides keep it neat.

### Side part

A classic, versatile cut. It shows the forehead and suits both business and casual settings.

### Crop or textured crop

Short, easy to style, and shows off a balanced face. A light fringe forward adds some character without hiding the forehead.

### Slick back

Works well if hair is thick enough. It shows the whole face, so it suits men comfortable with proportions being visible.

### Medium length with movement

A few inches of wavy or straight hair, swept back or to the side, looks relaxed. Keep it clean at the neckline.

## Fades and sides

Almost any fade works. A low or mid fade is a safe choice, while a high fade is more dramatic. Go for the one that matches your style and how often you can get a trim.

## Beards and oval faces

Oval faces suit most beards. A short, well-groomed beard or stubble works. A very long beard can extend the face. If you want to add a bit of jaw definition, keep the cheek line clean and the neckline tidy.

## Things that matter more than shape

Face shape is a small part of the picture. These things often matter more:

- **Hair texture and density:** Work with what you have. Fine, straight hair needs different cuts from thick, curly hair.
- **Hairline:** A receding hairline usually looks best with shorter hair. For hair loss concerns, ask a doctor or dermatologist.
- **Upkeep:** A great cut that grows out badly within two weeks is not great. Choose something that ages well.
- **Styling product:** A small amount of matte paste or clay is enough for most styles.

## Getting it right at the barber

1. Take two or three photos of cuts you like.
2. Describe the length on top and sides.
3. Ask for a style that grows out well.
4. Wait a few days before judging, since fresh cuts look sharp or stiff.
5. Photograph the result under normal light.
